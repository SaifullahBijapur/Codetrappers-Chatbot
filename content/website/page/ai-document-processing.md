1.  ### Classify before extracting

    Knowing what a document is decides which fields to look for. Skipping this step is why generic extraction produces a field called "total" holding a date.

2.  ### Extract against a schema

    Every field declared with a type and a validation rule, so an invoice date that is not a date fails loudly rather than flowing into your ledger.

3.  ### Score confidence, then route

    High confidence goes straight through. Low confidence goes to a person with the source document open beside the extracted values. That routing rule is the whole economics of the project.

4.  ### Write into the system of record

    Extraction that ends in a spreadsheet has moved the work rather than removed it. It has to land in the ERP, the accounting system or the CRM to count.

5.  ### Measure against a labelled set

    A few hundred documents with known correct answers, so accuracy is a number you can hold us to rather than an impression after a good demo.

## Why document projects disappoint

The technology is rarely the problem. These three are.

### The last five percent costs more than the first ninety-five

Any tool demos well on clean documents. Your real pile contains a photograph of a fax. Price the exceptions or the project overruns.

### Nobody agreed what correct means

Without a labelled set, accuracy is an argument rather than a measurement, and every disagreement becomes a meeting.

### There is no review path

Straight-through processing with no human route for the uncertain cases either blocks the queue or writes bad data into your ledger. Neither is recoverable cheaply.

## Reconciliation for finance, procurement and logistics

### Invoice and purchase order matching

Three-way matching between the invoice, the purchase order and the goods received note, with the mismatches routed to a person and the rest posted automatically. Tolerances are yours to set: quantity, price, currency and date.

### Supplier document validation

Checking that what arrived is what was agreed: the right entity, the right tax registration, the right terms, and an alert when a supplier quietly changes bank details, which is the most expensive kind of document fraud.

### Receipt and expense capture

Photographs, PDFs and email attachments turned into coded expense lines, with the VAT or GST split out where it can be established from the document rather than guessed.

### Duplicate and anomaly detection

The same invoice arriving twice under different references, an amount outside the normal range for that supplier, a purchase order with no matching delivery. Flagged before payment rather than found during an audit.

### Contract clause extraction

Renewal dates, notice periods, price escalation terms and liability caps pulled into something searchable, so a renewal is a calendar entry rather than a surprise.

### Approval workflow and system of record

Confident documents post straight through, uncertain ones queue for a named approver with the source alongside, and the result lands in the ERP or the accounting system rather than a spreadsheet.
