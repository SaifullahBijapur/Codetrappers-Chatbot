1.  ### Find where the money actually is

    A discovery workshop with the people who do the work, not only the people who sponsor it. What takes hours, what gets re-keyed between systems, what nobody has time to check. That list is where the value sits, and it rarely matches the list the executive team arrives with.

2.  ### Check the data before promising anything

    Most programmes fail on access rather than on models: information spread across systems that do not talk, with no interface anything could safely use. We establish that early, because it changes the plan and the budget more than any model choice will.

3.  ### Write a roadmap somebody can defend

    Each candidate ranked by value against effort, with an estimate, a payback and an honest note on what could go wrong. Including the ones we recommend not doing, which is usually the section that gets quoted back to us.

4.  ### Build the first one narrow

    One use case, real data, a fixed scope, measured against how the job is done today. If it does not beat that, it does not get rolled out, and we will say so rather than expand the pilot until it looks successful.

5.  ### Hand it over properly

    Documentation, an audit trail, and training for the people who use it and the people who maintain it. A system only your supplier understands is a dependency you did not agree to buy.

## What we tell enterprises that a slide deck will not

Three things decide whether a programme like this works, and none of them are the model you pick.

### Much of it is not AI

A large share of what gets asked for is a scheduled query nobody has time to run. That is cheaper, faster and more reliable built without a model, and we will point it out rather than sell you the more expensive version.

### Access is the bottleneck, not intelligence

The hard part is giving software safe, permissioned access to systems that were never designed to be read by anything but a person. That is engineering, and it is where the schedule really goes.

### Read-only earns the trust that write access needs

Programmes that start by letting an agent change things produce something nobody dares leave running. Start with reporting and surfacing, prove it, then grant more.
