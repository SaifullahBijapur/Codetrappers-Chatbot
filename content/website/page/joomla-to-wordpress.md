## What survives the move, and what does not

Most migration disputes come from this list being assumed rather than agreed. So we agree it first.

### Comes across

-   Articles, mapped to posts or pages
-   Categories and their nesting
-   Registered users
-   Media from the Joomla images directory

### Gets rebuilt

-   The template, including module positions
-   Every third-party extension
-   Menus, which Joomla models as content routes
-   K2 or VirtueMart data, which needs bespoke handling

The expensive surprises

## Three things specific to Joomla

### K2 content is not Joomla content

Sites on K2 store articles in their own tables entirely. A standard Joomla importer will report success and quietly bring across nothing.

### SEF URLs vary by configuration

Two Joomla sites can produce completely different URL shapes depending on settings and which SEF extension is installed. The live URLs get crawled and mapped, rather than assumed.

### Menus carry structure, not just links

In Joomla the menu item decides how a page renders. That logic has to be re-expressed as templates, which is easy to miss when only the labels are compared.

## Five steps, same every time

1.  1

    ### Crawl what is live

    Every URL currently indexed, exported to a sheet. This becomes the redirect map and the definition of done.

2.  2

    ### Audit the export

    What the platform gives us, compared against what the crawl found. The difference is the rebuild, and it gets priced before we start.

3.  3

    ### Build on staging

    The new site goes up somewhere private, with real content imported, so you review the actual thing rather than a mockup.

4.  4

    ### Map every redirect

    Old URL to new URL, one row at a time, tested as 301s on staging before anything points at production.

5.  5

    ### Cut over and watch

    DNS moves, then we monitor crawl errors and traffic for the fortnight that follows, because that is when problems surface.
