1.  ### Work out what genuinely cannot leave

    Usually it is less than people assume, and the answer changes the budget by an order of magnitude. Some workloads can use a hosted model with a zero-retention agreement; only the rest need hardware.

2.  ### Match the model to the hardware, not the headline

    An open-weight model that fits your GPU and answers your actual questions beats a larger one that needs a cluster and still has to be evaluated.

3.  ### Keep retrieval local as well

    Self-hosting the model and then sending your documents to a hosted embedding API defeats the purpose. The index belongs on the same side of the boundary.

4.  ### Prove it against a real test set

    The question is never whether a private model works, it is whether it works well enough for your task. That is measurable, and we measure it before you buy hardware.

5.  ### Plan for the upgrade you will want in a year

    Open weights improve quickly. A deployment that cannot swap the model without rebuilding everything around it ages badly.

## What self-hosting actually costs

We would rather you heard this now than after the purchase order, so here is the case against.

### There is a quality gap, and it is task-dependent

For retrieval and extraction over your own documents it is often small enough not to matter. For open-ended reasoning it is usually still real. Which of those you are doing decides whether this is a good idea.

### Somebody has to operate it

Patching, monitoring, capacity, and being awake when it stops. That is a running cost, and it is the one most often left out of the comparison.

### The treadmill does not stop

A model chosen today will be behind in a year. Budget for revisiting it rather than treating the deployment as finished.
