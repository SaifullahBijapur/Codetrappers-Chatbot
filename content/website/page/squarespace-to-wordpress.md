# Moving from Squarespace to WordPress

Squarespace exports pages and posts but omits products, galleries and index pages. We audit the gap before quoting, then move the lot.

Squarespace does export a WordPress-format file, which helps, but it quietly leaves out several content types. The gap between what the export contains and what your site shows is the whole job.

Before anyone quotes

## What survives the move, and what does not

Most migration disputes come from this list being assumed rather than agreed. So we agree it first.

### Comes across

-   Pages and blog posts, through the WordPress XML export
-   Images embedded in that content
-   Basic SEO titles and descriptions
-   Comments, where you had them

### Gets rebuilt

-   Layout, since Squarespace blocks have no WordPress equivalent
-   Product pages: Commerce data is not in the export
-   Album, gallery and index pages
-   Forms, scheduling and member areas

The expensive surprises

## Three things specific to Squarespace

### The export is narrower than it looks

It carries one blog, pages and text. Products, galleries, album pages and index pages are simply absent. Auditing what is missing before quoting is the difference between a fixed price and an argument.

### Dated blog URLs

Squarespace blog paths often carry the publish date. Match the permalink structure or write the redirects, but decide deliberately rather than discovering it after launch.

### Images point back at Squarespace

Content comes across referencing their CDN. Left alone, your images disappear the day the subscription lapses. Every asset gets pulled local and relinked.

How it runs

## Five steps, same every time

1.  1

    ### Crawl what is live

    Every URL currently indexed, exported to a sheet. This becomes the redirect map and the definition of done.

2.  2

    ### Audit the export

    What the platform gives us, compared against what the crawl found. The difference is the rebuild, and it gets priced before we start.

3.  3

    ### Build on staging

    The new site goes up somewhere private, with real content imported, so you review the actual thing rather than a mockup.

4.  4

    ### Map every redirect

    Old URL to new URL, one row at a time, tested as 301s on staging before anything points at production.

5.  5

    ### Cut over and watch

    DNS moves, then we monitor crawl errors and traffic for the fortnight that follows, because that is when problems surface.
