## What survives the move, and what does not

Most migration disputes come from this list being assumed rather than agreed. So we agree it first.

### Comes across

-   Page text and images, pulled page by page
-   Blog posts, via the RSS feed
-   Your domain, pointed at the new host
-   Page titles and meta descriptions

### Gets rebuilt

-   The entire design: Wix markup is proprietary and does not port
-   Forms, and wherever their submissions were going
-   Anything from the Wix App Market
-   Wix Stores, Bookings and Members areas

The expensive surprises

## Three things specific to Wix

### There is no export button

Wix does not let you take a database with you. Anyone promising a one-click move is describing a scrape. Done carefully that is fine, but it is a content extraction and should be priced as one.

### The URLs do not line up

Wix puts blog posts under /post/ and pages in a flat structure. Without a redirect map written before launch, every ranking page returns a 404 on day one.

### ADI layouts map to nothing

Sites built by Wix ADI have no structure underneath, just absolutely positioned blocks. Rebuilding is genuinely faster than trying to translate it.

## Five steps, same every time

1.  1

    ### Crawl what is live

    Every URL currently indexed, exported to a sheet. This becomes the redirect map and the definition of done.

2.  2

    ### Audit the export

    What the platform gives us, compared against what the crawl found. The difference is the rebuild, and it gets priced before we start.

3.  3

    ### Build on staging

    The new site goes up somewhere private, with real content imported, so you review the actual thing rather than a mockup.

4.  4

    ### Map every redirect

    Old URL to new URL, one row at a time, tested as 301s on staging before anything points at production.

5.  5

    ### Cut over and watch

    DNS moves, then we monitor crawl errors and traffic for the fortnight that follows, because that is when problems surface.
